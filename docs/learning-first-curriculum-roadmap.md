# NederUrdu Learning-First Curriculum Constitution

Status: **permanent curriculum source of truth**

This constitution governs every A0, A1, and A2 lesson, review, mission, content
generator, interface, and audit. A course change is incomplete when it conflicts
with this document, even if the app still runs or the learner can finish the
screen sequence.

NederUrdu follows an action-oriented, practical approach: learners first
understand useful Dutch, then practise it with support, and only then use it
independently. The curriculum is Urdu-first and intended for absolute beginners.
Its practical direction follows the
[CEFR action-oriented approach](https://book.coe.int/en/education-and-modern-languages/8150-common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching-assessment-companion-volume.html).
A2 includes listening, reading, speaking, and writing in line with the
[official Dutch A2 skill areas](https://inburgeren.nl/examen-doen/inhoud-taalexamens-a2-b1-b2.jsp),
without turning the course into exam coaching.

## 1. Required chapter contract

Every chapter must define:

- one or more real-life outcomes the learner will be able to perform;
- required earlier skills;
- genuinely new words, phrases, sounds, patterns, and communication functions;
- a dependency map proving that assessment follows introduction;
- units that move from foundations to practical use and end in a capstone
  mission;
- reviews that contain only previously introduced material; and
- a completion check covering meaning, listening, reading, supported speaking,
  and practical use.

All lessons remain browseable. A prerequisite may produce guidance and an
optional refresher, but it must not hide the lesson.
“Browseable” means the learner can always open the map entry and preview;
starting a mission may still wait until its declared skills are practiced.

## 2. Required lesson contract

Every normal lesson session is one continuous learning experience in this exact
order. When a large path lesson is divided into several capped internal runs,
each run must independently follow the same order and the learner completes one
coherent run at a time:

### Preview

- State one practical outcome in simple Urdu.
- In a multi-run lesson, state the outcome of the active run only. Do not name
  targets that will first appear in a later run.
- List the new material, known material, and prerequisites.
- Show estimated learning time and the lesson phases, never a question quota.

### Learn

- Give every new target its own teaching card.
- Show large Dutch text, Urdu meaning, Urdu pronunciation help, regular and slow
  audio, context or a useful visual, and at least one useful example.
- Sound and letter targets may be isolated for pronunciation. A normal word
  needs a natural phrase, sentence, contrast, or authentic micro-utterance;
  showing the word alone beside a picture is teaching support, not its example.
- Teach grammar from a real-life sentence: highlight the pattern, explain it in
  simple Urdu, contrast it with a nearby form, and show a common mistake.
- Do not score, quiz, or require guessing in this phase.

### Understand

- Demonstrate each exercise format before asking for an answer.
- Give every new target at least one model and one supported recognition
  activity.
- Prefer Dutch-to-Urdu meaning, matching, images, and listening with visible
  help.

### Guided Practice

- Move gradually into Urdu-to-Dutch recall, fill-gap, ordering, short dialogue,
  and supported sentence building.
- Keep meanings, pronunciation, examples, word banks, and hints available.
- Never request production until the same skill has been taught and recognised.

### Use

- Apply the material in a believable situation such as an appointment, shop,
  school, transport, doctor, work, form, or message.
- Give a concrete setting and action. Translation prompts, exercise
  instructions disguised as situations, generic topic prose, and generated or
  malformed sentence fragments are not practical use.
- Use only material taught in this lesson or marked as a secure prerequisite.

### Independent Check

- Ask four to six focused items without automatic hints.
- Cover every required lesson skill.
- Never introduce new content, instructions, or exercise formats here.

### Correction and Recap

- Explain why an answer is correct and why an incorrect answer is incorrect.
- For a choice task, explain the learner's selected distractor specifically;
  repeating the correct answer or one shared “try again” template is not an
  explanation.
- After an error, reteach the relevant meaning or rule and require one supported
  correction.
- End with what was learned, unresolved targets, and the next recommended
  review. Score and XP are secondary.

## 3. Content and accessibility limits

| Level | Maximum new material in one run |
| --- | --- |
| A0 | Four targets, or three targets and one small pattern |
| A1 | Five targets and one reusable sentence pattern |
| A2 | Four practical chunks and one grammar or communication function |

These limits are hard caps. New material must not be hidden in distractors,
documents, examples, instructions, reviews, or missions.

Additional non-negotiable rules:

- Every exercise has a specific Urdu instruction authored for that task.
- Every assessed exercise has a hint plus separate correct and incorrect
  explanations.
- Every exercise has stable semantic ownership through concept and skill IDs.
- Question IDs and semantic keys describe the authored learning task. They
  remain stable when order or surrounding copy changes; a hash of mutable
  wording alone is not a durable semantic identity.
- Urdu pronunciation help must be pronounceable and manually reviewed. A field
  that is merely non-empty, begins with a detached diacritic, or is mangled
  transliteration does not satisfy this requirement.
- Speaking repetition is an unscored self-check.
- Every typed response has an accessible word-bank alternative.
- Teaching is content, not a quiz question, and does not count toward assessment
  totals.

## 4. Mastery, reviews, and missions

A skill or lesson moves through three states:

- **introduced** after its Learn material has been viewed;
- **practiced** only after a correct Guided Practice or Use response for that
  skill, or a successful supported Correction tied to it; and
- **secure** only after at least 80% on the Independent Check and successful
  correction of every missed required skill.

Completing screens alone never creates practiced or secure mastery.

Reviews may draw only from introduced, practiced, or secure skills. Missions may
assess only practiced or secure prerequisite skills. Duplicate review lessons
must not appear as path nodes; spaced review is assembled adaptively by skill.
An introduced-only skill may receive supported recognition in adaptive review,
but not recall or production. Review success updates its spaced-review history;
it does not promote mastery. A skill becomes practiced only through its
lesson's Guided Practice and Use phases, and becomes secure only through the
Independent Check plus required correction.

A chapter completion mission must sample a representative assessed skill from
every chapter unit. Every playable variant must contain actual evidence for
meaning, listening, reading, unscored speaking support, and practical use;
declaring those labels in metadata is not evidence.

Missions contain no new teaching and therefore use the shorter exact sequence
**Preview → Use → Independent Check → Correction**. Preview is the visible
mission preview screen, not an injected generic “Learn” card. The playable
session begins with its authored practical Use record, keeps the chosen
variant's semantic IDs unchanged, and introduces no surprise format or phrase.

## 5. Chapter roadmap

### A0 — Understand and survive

Start with greetings, help phrases, yes/no, essential sounds, and immediately
useful chunks. Continue through identity, pronouns, being and having, numbers,
time, address, family, home, food, shopping, transport, health, school, work,
and safety. Teach sound, meaning, and context before spelling or grammar labels.
Keep sentences short and concrete, never require free typing, and end every unit
with a small mission that uses taught material only.

### A1 — Communicate in everyday life

Build reusable patterns around personal information, family, routines,
questions, appointments, home, food, transport, health, school, and work.
Introduce word order and verb changes through familiar phrases. Add short
dialogues, authentic forms, notices, messages, and supported speaking in the
normal lesson journey. Replace duplicate review path nodes with skill-based
spaced review. Unit missions combine secure skills without surprise language.

### A2 — Handle practical situations independently

Organise grammar around practical needs: gemeente and forms, employment, school
contact, health, housing, complaints, bills, banking, formal messages, past
events, and future plans. Teach one communication function and its grammar
inside a real situation. Increase document length, listening length, short
writing, and independent responses gradually. A target-only translation card
styled as a “document” is not document reading; use recognisable forms, notices,
messages, schedules, bills, and letters with progressively richer fields and
text. End units with capstone missions and offer optional prerequisite
refreshers.

## 6. Course data contract

`window.NEDERURDU_COURSE` is the canonical versioned course object. It contains
concepts, skills, chapters, units, lessons, and missions.
`window.NEDERURDU_CHAPTERS` may remain temporarily as a compatibility alias.

Inside a normal lesson, `exercises` is the only active v4 exercise collection.
While older code is being removed, `questions` may remain only as an exact
alias of that active collection. The former fixed question bank belongs in
`legacyQuestions`, is retired compatibility data, and may be read only for
one-time migration or recovery. A legacy record must never be selected into a
lesson run, review, or mission. Every retained legacy ID needs an explicit,
deterministic mapping to its active semantic exercise or owned skill so saved
v3 evidence cannot silently disappear.

Every concept includes Dutch, Urdu, pronunciation help, audio text, a useful
visual or context, concept-specific usage, examples, and a common confusion
grounded by either a concrete contrast concept or a specific Urdu usage
boundary. Repeating one generator sentence with only the quoted target changed
does not create authored usage or confusion teaching. Every exercise includes:

- a stable semantic ID;
- a durable semantic key when the ID contains an opaque compatibility or
  content hash;
- its canonical lesson phase;
- concept and skill ownership;
- a specific Urdu instruction and a hint; and
- separate correct and incorrect explanations, including an Urdu explanation
  for each selectable wrong option.

The same Dutch surface may have separate noun, verb, or contextual senses.
Those senses need separate stable semantic keys and teaching records; one
record must not combine incompatible meanings. Translation aliases must be
genuine equivalents, not broad categories such as accepting “document” for
“passport.”

Progress is stored under `nederurdu-progress-v4`. One-time migration from v3
preserves settings, XP, practice days, lesson history, mistakes, and mission
history; maps a previously completed lesson to **practiced**, never
automatically **secure**; and leaves the v3 record intact as a recovery backup.

Web and Android course data, assets, styling, progress behaviour, and offline
support must remain synchronized.

## 7. Mandatory chapter delivery cycle

Complete and freeze A0 before changing A1; complete and freeze A1 before
changing A2. For each chapter:

1. Inventory every lesson, concept, phrase, exercise, and prerequisite.
2. Decide what remains, splits, moves, merges, or retires.
3. Author complete Urdu-first concept and teaching records.
4. Map exercises to stable concept and skill IDs.
5. Generate sessions in the required learning phases.
6. Audit every generated item and manually review the learner journey.
7. Record the results and freeze the chapter before starting the next one.

## 8. Acceptance gates

The generated-course audit must reject:

- missing chapter contracts or real-life outcomes;
- exercises without concept and skill ownership;
- assessed material without earlier teaching or a secure prerequisite;
- production before supported recognition;
- a run above its level's new-material cap;
- unseen material in reviews or missions;
- missing Urdu instructions, pronunciation, examples, hints, or explanations;
- generic or mangled Use contexts;
- generic feedback and missing selected-distractor explanations;
- copied lesson-wide outcomes that preview targets from a later internal run;
- target-only “documents,” missing A1 practical reading, or A2 documents that
  do not grow in complexity;
- completion missions that omit a unit or declare a skill area without an
  actual exercise providing that evidence;
- malformed pronunciation help and opaque content-hash identities without a
  durable semantic key;
- duplicate review lessons; and
- mission assessment skills that are not mission prerequisites.

Browser tests must cover every phase, the preview and prerequisite guidance,
correct and incorrect feedback, supported correction, mastery transitions,
review filtering, mission prerequisites, and v3-to-v4 migration.

Each chapter must reach **zero errors and zero review flags**. Representative
beginning, middle, and final lessons must also pass phone, tablet, and desktop
visual review. Audio, slow audio, Urdu typography, visuals, offline use, Android
parity, Android update persistence, and a native debug build are release gates.
No chapter is complete while any gate remains unresolved.
